I ran my own harness and it failed its own central claim

Apoapsis is a substrate for coding agents. Models propose; the harness controls files, commands, verification and completion. Then I measured it properly and found that six of seven completed work units could not point at the evidence that justified completing them.

Findings published 22 containment probes, 0 breaches 6 of 7 slices could not locate their evidence Local and frontier routing
01The premise

A green tick has to be traceable to a run

Coding agents are confident. They report success on work that does not pass, and the reporting layer is usually the model's own summary of what it thinks it did. Apoapsis inverts that. The model proposes changes and nothing else. Files, commands, verification, retries, state transitions and the decision that work is complete all belong to the harness, recorded in SQLite and Git.

The design claim is one sentence: a completion mark must be traceable to a verification run the harness performed itself. Everything in the product exists to make that checkable.

02The record

Specification before code

Every task starts as a specification with the user's exact words preserved next to a deterministic interpretation and a verification method. Hard constraints stay verbatim, because paraphrasing a constraint is how you lose it. Acceptance criteria carry the check that would prove them.

03The measurement

What happened when I actually looked

In August I ran three live attempts at one work slice while preparing a demo, then went back and read the evidence for the seven slices before it. I was not looking for defects. I found this:

Six of seven promoted slices could no longer point at the evidence that justified promoting them. That is the one thing the product exists to do. The harness was honest about it, which is the reporting layer working as designed, and an evidence trail that reports missing for 86% of completed work is still not an evidence trail.

The part I want you to notice

SLICE-007, the one the demo was built on, is the only intact one. That was luck. I picked it for its verdict text before I looked at integrity. I wrote that down in the findings document rather than quietly shipping the demo, because a demo standing on the single healthy sample is exactly the result that needs a footnote.

  • The checkpoint said CONTINUE on 761 lines of plausible code because the unit-test witness was not current and fingerprint-bound. The harness refusing to call plausible code done is the behaviour I built it for.
  • The relay refused to score truncated output rather than accept partial responses, which is why the run surfaces as an honest escalation instead of a false completion.
  • The stale-source guard fired on me. It refused to run at all with uncommitted changes in the product code: the authority boundary defending itself against its own author.
  • A 4.5× measurement confound found and named. Same model, same flags, 9–14 tokens/sec with a busy desktop against 42.6 on a quiet one. Any benchmark that ignores that is measuring desktop load.
05Why this is the case study I lead with

Being wrong on the record

It would have been easy to write a page saying Apoapsis routes work between local and frontier models, keeps a full audit trail, and beats an unharnessed local model. All of that is true.

It would also have skipped the finding that matters. I built a system whose entire argument is verifiable completion, measured it, and found the verification trail broken across most of its own history. Then I ordered the fix ahead of the roadmap priorities I had already written, and recorded that one of the six could be genuinely absent evidence and the other five merely unlinked, because the current message cannot tell those apart and pretending it can would be a second error.

If you are hiring someone to evaluate AI systems, that is the work sample. Anyone can report a green run.
